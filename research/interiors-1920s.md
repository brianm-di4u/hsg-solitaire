# Hot Springs interiors, 1910s–1930s: materials, colors, motifs

Research for the lit tabletop and the Art Deco card art in Spa City Solitaire. Every line below comes from
a cited source. Tags:

- **[confirmed]**: a primary or official source (a period newspaper or booklet quoted in an NPS report, an
  NPS or HABS document, or a National Register nomination), or two or more independent sources.
- **[single-source]**: one secondary source only.
- **[observed]**: what a public-domain HABS photograph shows (Jack E. Boucher, June 1984). No text source
  backs it. The color transparencies have a warm tungsten cast, so judge hue families from them, not exact
  values.

"Not found" means it was searched for and no source turned up. This file gives no hex values. The shared
estimates stay in `research/visual-style-guide.md`. The **Conflicts with shared research** section lists
every place where these sources disagree with the corpus.

## 1. Fordyce Bathhouse (opened March 1, 1915)

### Men's bath hall, DeSoto fountain and skylight

- Arrangement: thirty 7×10 ft bath rooms "arranged around the room so as to form a court in the center about 50 feet square" (*Hot Springs New Era*, Feb. 27, 1915, quoted in HFR). [confirmed] HFR
- Booklet wording (*Fordyce Bath House*, c.1915, p.14): a "large art glass dome containing approximately eight thousand pieces of glass worked into a design representing Neptune's daughter, mermaids and sea fish in a swirl of water." [confirmed] HFR; also quoted in HSR p.109
- Newspaper wording (*Sentinel-Record*, Feb. 28, 1915): "a man, a mermaid and a woman, together with sea fish of divers kind, in a swirl or whirlpool of water ... they can scarcely believe but what it is a magnificent painting." [confirmed] HFR
- *Arkansas Gazette*, Feb. 28, 1915: "a large central skylight of art glass sheds a diffused light ... fish, frogs, mermaids and human bathers can be seen swimming around above." [confirmed] HFR
- Piece count: "approximately 18,000 pieces" (*New Era*, Feb. 27, 1915) vs "approximately eight thousand" (booklet c.1915). Sources conflict. [confirmed that both are printed] HFR
- **Maker:** *The Ornamental Glass Bulletin* (Chicago), Feb. 1917, captions a picture of this skylight "Designed and Executed by CONDIE-BRAY GLASS AND PAINT CO., ST. LOUIS, MO., FOR CEILING LIGHT OVER POND, FORDYCE BATHHOUSE." [confirmed] HFR (reference 12 under "Men's Bath Hall"). A 1982 letter from C. Powell Fordyce takes back the family's Tiffany attribution in favor of this journal. HFR
- The earlier HSR (pp. 109–111) called the maker "not documented" and floated Binswanger Glass (Memphis) and Tiffany. The 1917 citation in the HFR supersedes that. [confirmed] HSR, HFR
- NPS podcast transcript (2015) credits "the Condi-Neal Glass Company from St. Louis" with the **Music/Assembly Room** glass (and the ladies' bath hall and pack room), not the men's hall. [single-source] https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/photosmultimedia/fordyce-stained-glass.htm
- Skylight frame paint: the dome moulding "is highly tinted in colors to match the art glass" (*Maintaining a Standard*, O'Brien Paint Co., c.1916). A 1930s employee recalled that "each section of the molding around the stained glass skylight was painted a different color". [confirmed] HFR
- **Skylight composition:** an outer frame of pale amber/honey-yellow opalescent rectangular panes. Scallop-shell roundels sit in dark-leaded circles: three on each long side and one in each corner. Inside is a large oval of blue, teal and green "water" with leafy weed lines and bubbles. On the oval are a mermaid with a gray fish tail, two swimming women, fish and a crab-like creature. A cross of lead lines divides a pale-yellow inner oval, and a small rose/lavender whirlpool sits in the center. Blue-and-white scroll spandrels fill the corners between the oval and the frame. [observed] HABS AR-28-D color transparency, LOC file 570634 (`fordyce-mens-bath-skylight-detail-color-1984.jpg`)
- DeSoto fountain: "a giant statue fountain representing de Soto standing erect in his Spanish costume, helmet and lance, receiving a drink of water from an Indian maiden" (*Sentinel-Record*, Feb. 28, 1915). [confirmed] HFR
- Statue material: "a beautiful piece of statuary, in American bisque". The base was to be "of verdi antique marble" (undated clipping c.1915–16, Hudgins scrapbook). [single-source, primary clipping] HFR
- Fountain supply: the design came from the American Tile Company (letter, Jan. 19, 1915). It was billed through the Southwestern Tile Co. at $750 (Dec. 1915); the 1918 ledger lists "De Soto Fountain $988.01". [confirmed] HFR
- Installed date: not installed at the March 1, 1915 opening. It went into the bath hall "sometime between December 13, 1915, and March 1, 1916". [confirmed] HFR
- Marble benches: "magnificent hand-carved marble lounges or benches built in the Roman style" (1915). The ledger lists "Marble Benches $602.80". [confirmed] HFR
- Floor (1984): white small-tile mosaic dotted with a regular grid of small dark tiles. Fountain basin rim of amber/olive glazed tile with a green-tiled pool. Low veined-marble partitions (white to warm gray) carry reddish-brown wood stall doors, with nickel-colored pipe rails above. [observed] HABS AR-28-D color transparency (LOC file 570635) and AR-28-D-20

### Marble, tile and stone (whole building)

- Lobby walls: "covered by the most beautifully veined Italian Marble" (c.1915 press release; *Gazette*, Feb. 28, 1915). [confirmed] HFR
- *New Era*, Feb. 27, 1915: "The walls of this lobby are of English-veined Italian marble." [confirmed] HFR
- Booklet c.1925–30 (*Health Awaits You at the Fordyce Baths*, p.14): bath-hall "floors, walls and partitions of veined Vermont marble". [confirmed] HFR, HSR p.104–5
- Marble source in fact: WWI stopped the supplier (Southwestern Tile Co.) from buying imported marble in New Orleans, so John Fordyce found stock at the Peoria Marble Works, Illinois. One carload shipped from Peoria in Oct. 1914, and S.W. Fordyce complained it was not "the right kind of marble". [confirmed] HFR ("1912–1915 Planning and Construction"); HSR p.105
- HSR verdict: "It is unclear exactly what kind or kinds of marble were employed." [confirmed] HSR p.104
- Stairs: "stately staircases of pink marble lead to the second floor and basement" (*Fordyce Bath House*, 1915, p.13). [confirmed] HFR; HSR
- Lobby floor: "ceramic tile, worked out in soft colors of green, yellow, red and black, in a field of white" (*Gazette*, Feb. 28, 1915). [confirmed] HFR; HSR
- *Sentinel-Record*, 1915: lobby floor and "all other floors throughout the house" are "of tile worked and of elaborate and appropriate designs". [confirmed] HFR
- Lobby floor pattern (1984): white mosaic field with rows of dark cross-crosslet (snowflake-like) motifs and a Greek-key (meander) border band. [observed] HABS AR-28-D-17, -18
- Lobby fountains: "At each end is a running fountain of hot water, Cupids and Shells of Faience tile all colored in soft neutral tints make a pleasing contrast to the grays of the marble". [confirmed] HFR; HSR
- Fountain form (1984): a square-tile panel framed by fluted pilasters under a cornice with leafy arched panels. A cupid stands on a scallop shell over a bracket, above a curved stepped basin. [observed] HABS AR-28-D-18
- Bath rooms: "lined with white tile, marble partitions form the bath rooms around the walls"; tubs of porcelain (c.1915). [confirmed] HFR
- White tile wainscoting in the men's bath hall was "practically completed" by Oct. 20, 1914 (Superintendent Parks). [confirmed] HFR; HSR
- Front terrace: floored with red "promenade" tile (*Gazette*, 1915). [confirmed] HSR p.104
- Foundation: Batesville stone, "taking polish like marble" (*Sentinel-Record*, 1915). [confirmed] HSR p.104
- Exterior brick (1915): lower story of cream-colored enamel brick and tile. The second and third floors are "brown and light yellow pressed brick, the darker working out a diamond bond in front". [confirmed] HSR p.104 (citing *Gazette*)
- Terra cotta (1915): "shells, starfish, dolphins, mermaids, aquatic birds and plants. Over each main window was the head of Neptune". [confirmed] HSR p.104; NPS sheet (Fordyce) says "ivory-glazed window surrounds of sculpted terra cotta with the bust of Neptune"
- Terrazzo at the Fordyce: not found in any Fordyce source. Ceramic tile and marble are named instead. [not found]
- Mosaic Tile Co. supplied the 1939 Hubbard-tub tile, "prevailing color is light green" (J.R. Fordyce, Jan. 31, 1939). [confirmed] HFR

### Lobby glass, lighting and metalwork

- Transoms: "a series of Art Glass transoms, representing cool green pools of water on whose surface float beautiful water lillies" (c.1915 press release). [confirmed] HFR
- Lighting: "Alabaster bowls suspended from the ceiling cast a soft diffused light from the old ivory tints above, when the nitrogen filled Mazda lamps are turned on". [confirmed] HFR
- Fixtures: "The electrical fixtures are of oxidized silver" (*Gazette*, 1915), and "electrical fixtures and hardware trimmings are silver oxidized" (booklet, 1915). [confirmed] HFR
- The original lighting fixtures were removed and fluorescent lights installed in 1945. [confirmed] HFR
- Bath fittings "of solid nickel", each tub with a built-in thermometer (*New Era*, 1915). Cots of porcelain "with nickel legs". [confirmed] HFR; HSR p.119
- Valuables lockers: "solid bronze". The count is 245 (HSR p.119) vs 345 (*Sentinel-Record* as quoted in HFR). [confirmed material; count conflicts]
- Marquee: "copper and stained glass marquee suspended by chains" (*Gazette*, 1915). [confirmed] HSR p.109; NR

### Assembly / palm room ("Music Room" today), parlors, staterooms

- *Gazette*, Feb. 28, 1915: the Palm Room is "without a doubt the most beautiful in the whole house ... The direct overhead rays of the sun are filtered through soft tinted stained glass, so that the whole ceiling appears to be studded with jewels. The furniture is of art reeds, tinted gray and upholstered in gray leather." [confirmed] HFR
- Ceiling: "divided into five art glass panels, worked out in designs representing Music, Art, Science, etc." (*Sentinel-Record*, 1915). The room measured about 100 × 20 ft. [confirmed] HFR; HSR p.60
- NR: "segmentally arched vaults of the ceiling are filled in with arched, stained glass skylights". [confirmed] NR p.5
- NPS today calls this the Music Room: "patterned tile floor, stained glass ceiling and Knabe grand piano". [confirmed] https://home.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/fordyce-bathhouse.htm
- The NPS podcast lists instruments in the glass: "A violin, French horn, harp". [single-source] NPS podcast URL above
- Barrel-vaulted glass bays (1984): mostly pale amber/cream clear panes in a diagonal grid, each bay with a colored oval medallion (red, gold, blue). Floor of cream mosaic with red-brown and slate-blue star and cross medallions and a red-brown border. Cream walls with arched French doors, brass sconces and dark brown wicker chairs. [observed] HABS AR-28-D color transparency, LOC file 570633 (captioned "Reading room with stained glass ceiling")
- In 1915 the "music room" was the ladies' parlor at the south end (concert grand piano, Victrola). The gentlemen's parlor and billiard room at the north end held "a billiard and pool table". Both had "large open fireplaces" and "marble fountains for hot and cold water". [confirmed] HFR; HSR p.60
- Billiard table: a Feb. 1916 expense account lists a purchase from Brunswick-Balke-Collender of "1 set green pockets for billiard table". [single-source, primary record] HFR
- Staterooms: 22 rooms, 8×12 ft; furniture "of white enameled metal". [confirmed] HSR p.60, p.119
- Dressing rooms: 285 of "mahogany stained birch", with "apple green tinted walls" (*Gazette*, 1915). [confirmed] HSR p.119, p.125; HFR

### Gymnasium

- 1915: "undoubtedly the largest in the State of Arkansas", 70 × 30 ft, with turning poles, springboard, horizontal bars, punching bags, flying trapeze, traveling rings and vaulting horses. [confirmed] HSR p.70
- NR: "a wood panelled gymnasium to the rear" of the third floor. The NPS sheet calls it "a wood-paneled coed gymnasium". [confirmed] NR p.5; NPS sheet
- In 1984: a dark-stained board wainscot about 4 ft high, plaster walls above, exposed steel roof trusses under a board ceiling, a strip-wood floor and tall multi-pane windows. [observed] HABS AR-28-D-32

### Paint and woodwork

- Walls and ceilings were finished throughout with O'Brien's "Liquid Velvet" flat wall enamel in "most delicate tints", "a marvelous panorama of color and beauty" (c.1916). [confirmed] HSR p.125; HFR
- HFR: "Determining paint colors for the bathhouse interior is problematic". Only the apple-green dressing rooms are specified for 1915. [confirmed] HFR
- Lobby repainted "desert sand" c.1938–42 (interview, Glenn Spring). 1961 receipt: Sherwin-Williams "SK-1 Driftwood White". [confirmed] HFR; HSR p.125
- Lobby furniture 1915–1952: "Art reed furniture, tinted brown and upholstered in brown Spanish leather", with ferns and art pottery. It was replaced in 1952 with wrought iron painted "Pompeian green". [confirmed] HFR
- Basement Fordyce spring: "a glazed tile room with an arched ceiling and a plate glass window". The NPS sheet calls it a "quartz-crystal ornamented spring display". [confirmed] NR; NPS sheet

## 2. Arlington Hotel (third building, 1923–24)

- Dates: begun Oct. 25, 1923, completed Nov. 28, 1924. [single-source] https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/ ; the CAHD nomination says "built in 1925" (conflict, already noted in the corpus).
- Exterior (CAHD form HS-63): "Constructed of buff brick, the central portion of the building is eleven stories tall with two splayed seven story wings. Domed towers with balconies". [confirmed] CAHD
- The loggia is "stuccoed and trimmed with inset colored tile and a red tile parapet coping". The entrance has "yellow planters". The arcades carry "a carved stone panel and colored tile surrounding a sculpture encircled by a niche". [confirmed] CAHD

- A travel blog mentions "painted backdrops" and "tilework around the water fountain" in the lobby, and ceiling, chandeliers and floor in the Venetian Dining Room. Undated and unattributed. [single-source] https://www.tiedyetravels.com/2015/08/arlington-hotel.html

- The hotel's own history page captions a vintage photo with dancing "under a crystal chandelier in a grand ballroom" and names a "Fountain Room" for dining. [single-source] https://www.arlingtonhotel.com/history/

## 3. Other Row interiors

### Maurice (1911–12; remodeled 1915)

- Lobby (pre-1915 booklet): "finished in oak of the early English style, containing beautiful columns and panels' and with a red Ludiwicke [Ludowici] tile floor and marble base". [confirmed] Maurice booklet
- Bath room "finished in white tile with marble Tarrazo [terrazzo] floors"; bath-room partitions "of white marble". [confirmed] Maurice booklet
- Solarium (pre-1915): "glass and steel dome", walls of "French Cean [Caen] Stone", floor of red Ludowici tiling with a marble terrazzo base. [confirmed] Maurice booklet
- Floors throughout "are either marble Tarrazo, Ceramic or red tiling". Furnishings "of white steel enamel or hard wood" with no carpets or upholstery. [confirmed] Maurice booklet
- Sun parlor (*Cutter's Guide*, 1915): "The sun's glare through the massive glass dome is subdued by amber glass panels overhead, the walls of Caen stone, the floor of red Scotch tile. The soft amber glow is very beneficial and restful". [confirmed] HFR fig. 115
- NPS sheet: the 1915 remodel bought "large stained glass skylights from the Rossbach Art Glass Company in Columbus, Ohio (later of Chicago), along with hand-painted canvas wall coverings". [confirmed] NPS sheet (Maurice)
- Lobby: "lined with gilded oak pilasters with ionic capitals". NR: "arches and fluted Ionic pilasters", plus the "orange neon 'Maurice' sign on the wall behind the marble counter". [confirmed] NPS sheet; NR
- Lobby (1984): "THE MAURICE" spelled in dark tiles across a white mosaic floor dotted with small dark crosses. The marble desk counter has the "MAURICE" letter sign above it, and there are painted ornamental ceiling beams. [observed] HABS AR-28-C-26
- Art glass: "Stained glass skylights and windows of mythical sea scenes in the men's and women's portions" (NR). The women's pack-room windows photographed in 1984 show opalescent pale panes, a green vertical stripe, an amber border, and a white water lily on green pads with a blue-and-rose creature above it. [confirmed text: NR; observed: HABS AR-28-C-42]
- **Roycroft ("Dutch") Den** (*Cutter's Guide*, 1916): "25 x 60 ft., red tile floor, walnut wainscotting with art glass panel ceiling. The freize picture 'Holland Life' is by the well known artist, Frederick Warnecke ... cobblestone fireplace ... comfortable Roycroft furnishings ... croquet billiard table". [confirmed] HFR fig. 119
- The NPS sheet names the same frieze "Dutch Life" by "Frederick Wernicke" and adds a "stained glass clerestory, and plaster Bacchus mascarons". The NR says "dark-panelled ... inglenook fireplace with flanking benches. Carved mascarons". [confirmed; title and artist spelling conflict]
- A second-floor billiard room "with a mural". [confirmed] NR
- "Roman bath" room at the Maurice: **not found**. Sources mention a 1931 therapeutic pool and a Nauheim bath only.

### Quapaw (1922)

- Dome: "covered with brilliantly colored tiles and capped with a small copper cupola. The dome's mosaic is chevron-patterned with a band of rectangular and diamond patterns encircling its base. The dome rests on an octagonal base". [confirmed] NR
- HABS caption: the octagonal base "houses a quatrefoil ventilator"; "an underground grotto in the basement from which a hot spring emerges". [confirmed] HABS AR-28-E caption page (ar0036cap.pdf)
- **Dome colors (1984 color photo):** the chevron field is buff/tan and cream zigzags with small gold-and-blue diamond accents. The base band is ochre/gold diamond tiles alternating with gold rosettes, edged in brown stripes. Set into the band are turquoise picture panels: a cream winged quadruped (griffin-like), and a diamond panel holding a mask-like face. The lowest border is ochre with ring motifs, and the lantern is dark bronze. [observed] HABS AR-28-E-18 color (`quapaw-dome-mosaic-lantern-color-1984.jpg`)
- Interior under the dome, and interior finishes generally: **not found**. NPS says the Quapaw "was more impressive in its exterior facade than in its interior appointments" (said of the Ozark "like the Quapaw"). [confirmed] NPS sheet (Ozark)

### Buckstaff (1912)

- "Colorado marble is used throughout the interior, particularly in the bath halls. The floors are of white and colored hexagonal tile in varying patterns", with an ornate original elevator. [confirmed] NPS sheet (Buckstaff)
- Sun parlor (*Buckstaff Baths* booklet, c.1915): "white tile floor with red figure, heavy dark green rugs, mahogany furniture with dark green leather trimmings, lace curtains, green and gold window draperies and white walls". It was "furnished in solid mahogany". [confirmed] HFR fig. 113
- Brass: "brass lettered words 'BUCKSTAFF BATHS'", brass handrails and "brass-covered and glazed wood frame entrance doors". [confirmed] NR
- Men's bath (1984): floor-to-ceiling veined white marble slabs, marble stall partitions on nickel legs, small white mosaic floor. [observed] HABS AR-28-G-5

### Ozark (1922)

- "the central lobby has a marble counter ... Mirrors cover the walls in the lobby. The floor of the sun porch is covered with quarry tile". [confirmed] NR
- Lobby floor (1984): mosaic of interlocking square and diamond outlines with small dark square centers, a stepped geometric lattice. [observed] HABS AR-28-F-8

### Superior (1916) and Lamar (1923)

- Superior: "Bath stalls are marble-walled with tile floors ... The front desk in the lobby is marble. Stairs are marble and tile. Most of the interior hardware is brass." Neon signs inside. [confirmed] NR
- Lamar: the lobby's "north, south, and east walls are covered with murals of architectural and country scenes". [confirmed] NR

## 4. Gaming clubs (1920s)

- Southern Club (250 Central): Romanesque Revival dressed-stone arcaded second floor with egg-and-dart moulding. The first-floor arches "were removed when the existing glass, metal and structural glass storefront and canopy were added". "The extensive remodeling to the first floor took place during the 1940's." [confirmed] CAHD form HS-21
- Southern Club (EoA): Jacobs bought it in 1927 and "encased the front of the building in dark Pittsburgh glass". "Most of the lighting fixtures in the building are original". Original "fixtures, furniture, wallpaper" survive. A "ten-and-a-half foot wide marble staircase" was added in the 1940s, and an escalator in the 1950s. Capone "always sat at an elevated table". [single-source] https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/southern-club-1208/
- Ohio Club (c.1905): "one of the only examples of a pressed metal facade dating from this period", a second-floor bay with a domed roof, built as "a private club with a saloon and restrained gambling". It was the Ohio Cigar Store during Prohibition. [confirmed] CAHD form HS-28
- Belvedere Club interior: **not found**. Only a magazine line calls it "a stately country club on rolling hills that could hold up to 2,000 gamblers". [single-source] https://www.theringer.com/2020/06/29/gambling/the-vapors-david-hill-arkansas-casino
- Card tables, felt color, table wood and club lighting in any 1920s Hot Springs club: **not found**.
- The nearest period-documented game furnishing is the Fordyce gentlemen's parlor billiard and pool table (1915) with "green pockets" bought from Brunswick-Balke-Collender in Feb. 1916. See section 1.

## 5. 1920s American playing cards (general background)

- USPCC lineage: Russell, Morgan & Co., Cincinnati, 1867. Four brands were launched in 1881, Congress being the most expensive. Bicycle No. 808 dates from 1885. The company became the United States Printing Co. in 1891, and the card business was spun off as The United States Playing Card Co. in 1894. [single-source] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Playing_Card_Company
- Congress came in bridge size from 1922. Aviator was introduced in 1927 for Lindbergh's flight. [single-source] same
- Card size: standardized in the 19th century "initially to a size of 3½ × 2½ inches" (poker or "wide"). Narrower "whist-sized" or "bridge-sized" cards exist, but "there is no formal requirement for precise adherence". [single-source] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_52-card_deck
- Jumbo indices: "introduced in 1895, and were given the subtitle '88'" (USPCC). [confirmed: 2 sources] https://wopc.co.uk/usa/the-united-states-playing-card-company/jumbo-bridge-88 ; https://www.amusedbyjokersami.com/2020/08/us-playing-card-company.html
- Corner indices: Squeezers (1870) were "the first cards with indices that had a large diffusion" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playing_card). Another Wikipedia article says corner indices were added "around 1880" (Standard 52-card deck). [conflict; both single-source]
- Rounded corners replaced sharp ones because sharp corners wore faster and could reveal the card. No date or radius is given. [single-source] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playing_card. **Standard corner radius: not found.**
- Courts: USPCC "US4" courts were "introduced in c.1900 and continued in use up until the 1950s". "US3 seems to have disappeared around 1930". The 1920s De Luxe and Bee Bridge Whist courts were "totally different", like those of the Swiss maker Müller. [single-source] https://www.wopc.co.uk/blogs/ken-lodge/united-states-playing-card-co
- Court coloring: after an 1845 fire, Goodall redrew its courts with "the prominent red, blue, and yellow colouring that persists today". Goodall "pioneered the widespread introduction of double-headed cards" in the 1860s. [single-source] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Goodall_%26_Sons
- Air-Cushion finish: USPCC is said to have filed a trademark application on Feb. 19, 1907, and advertised it as "new" until after 1910. Not verified against USPTO. [single-source] https://www.amusedbyjokersami.com/2022/12/air-cushion-cards-by-uspcc.html
- Linen finish: Goodall's "Linette" brand dates to Sept. 1923 and Waddington's "Silkette" to July 1925 (https://wopc.co.uk/members/roddy-somerville/waddingtons-silkette-brand). Wikipedia gives "linen grained cards in 1906, under the 'Linette' product name". [conflict; both single-source]
- A US example: a George B. Hurd & Co. deck catalogued "Gold Edges- Linen Finish", c.1920–28. [single-source] (UNLV Special Collections record, via search summary: https://special-delta.library.unlv.edu/node/764387)
- Russell- or Saks-branded Art Deco court designs, and typical 1920s card-stock color: **not found**.

## 6. Period color and material names (palette vocabulary)

All are period wording quoted above, [confirmed] unless marked.

- "veined Italian marble" / "English-veined Italian marble" / "veined Vermont marble": Fordyce lobby and baths, read as "the grays of the marble".
- "pink marble": Fordyce staircases.
- "verdi antique marble": DeSoto fountain base, Fordyce. This is the only verde antique on record here. [single-source clipping]
- "Colorado marble": Buckstaff (NPS sheet, modern wording).
- "soft colors of green, yellow, red and black, in a field of white": Fordyce ceramic floor.
- "Faience tile ... soft neutral tints": Fordyce lobby fountains.
- "cool green pools of water ... water lillies": Fordyce lobby transoms.
- "old ivory tints": Fordyce lobby ceiling. "oxidized silver" fixtures, "solid nickel" fittings, "solid bronze" lockers.
- "apple green": Fordyce dressing-room walls. "mahogany stained birch": dressing-room partitions.
- "art reeds, tinted gray ... gray leather" (assembly room); "tinted brown ... brown Spanish leather" (lobby).
- "soft tinted stained glass ... studded with jewels": Fordyce assembly-room ceiling.
- "red 'promenade' tile": Fordyce terrace. "Batesville stone": foundation.
- "brown and light yellow pressed brick ... diamond bond": Fordyce upper stories.

## Conflicts with shared research (flagged)

1. **Fordyce glass maker.** `visual-style-guide.md` (Fordyce) and `bathhouse-row-and-springs.md` say "The
   Condi-Neal Glass Company of St. Louis installed the glass in 1914–15, including Neptune's Daughter /
   mermaid imagery in the men's bath hall." The 1917 *Ornamental Glass Bulletin* credits the men's bath hall
   ceiling light to **Condie-Bray Glass and Paint Co., St. Louis** (HFR). The NPS podcast credits
   "Condi-Neal" only with the Music/Assembly Room and ladies' glass. Whether Condie-Bray and Condi-Neal are one
   firm under two names is **not established** by these sources. `town-content.md` back #1 caption ("Condi-Neal
   Glass, 1914–15") should be reworded, for example to "Condie-Bray Glass & Paint Co., St. Louis, per a 1917 trade
   journal".
2. **Mermaid room "[DISPUTED]".** The 1915 newspapers and booklets settle it. Mermaids, Neptune's daughter, fish,
   frogs and human bathers are in the **men's bath hall** skylight. The assembly/palm room ceiling has five panels
   for "Music, Art, Science, etc." (HFR). The corpus can drop the dispute flag.
3. **Piece count.** The corpus says "about 8,000 pieces" (VOA). Period sources print both "approximately eight
   thousand" (booklet) and "approximately 18,000" (*New Era*, Feb. 27, 1915).
4. **DeSoto fountain date and material.** The corpus lists the fountain as 1914–15 under the skylight. It was
   **installed between Dec. 13, 1915 and Mar. 1, 1916**, after opening. The statue is "American bisque" on a
   "verdi antique marble" base (HFR).
5. **Quapaw dome colors.** The corpus guesses cobalt, gold, terra cotta, green and cream chevrons. The 1984 HABS
   color photo shows a **buff/tan and cream chevron field with small gold-and-blue diamonds, an ochre/gold base
   band and turquoise pictorial panels**. Cobalt, terra cotta and green are not the main colors. The `quapaw`
   back palette and Motif 2 should be re-keyed to the photo.
6. **Southern Club glass date.** The corpus states "dark Pittsburgh glass (1927)". EoA ties the glass to Jacobs,
   who bought the club in 1927, but gives no glass date. The 1985 CAHD nomination says the structural-glass
   storefront and the "extensive remodeling to the first floor took place during the 1940's". Date the glass
   "after 1927 (possibly 1940s)".
7. **Maurice "mermaid art glass".** The corpus cites EoA. The NR says "mythical sea scenes", and the one Maurice
   window photographed in color (women's pack room) shows water lilies, not mermaids. The NPS names the 1915
   skylight maker as Rossbach Art Glass Co., Columbus, Ohio, which the corpus does not mention.
8. **Fordyce lozenge brick.** The corpus draws cream brick with a `#B89A6A` lozenge outline. The 1915 *Gazette*
   says "brown and light yellow pressed brick, the darker working out a diamond bond". So the lozenges are
   darker brown brick set in light-yellow brick, and only the first story is cream enamel brick.
9. **Music Room naming.** The corpus puts the "patterned tile floor, stained glass ceiling ... Knabe piano" in
   the third-floor Music Room, which is the NPS's current name. In 1915 the glass-ceilinged space was the
   **assembly or palm room**, and the "music room" was the ladies' parlor at its south end. Captions should
   say "assembly room (today's Music Room)".
10. Minor: the Maurice Den frieze is "Dutch Life" by Frederick Wernicke (NPS sheet) or "Holland Life" by
    Frederick Warnecke (*Cutter's Guide*, 1916). The Arlington completion date is 1924 (hotel) or 1925 (CAHD),
    already flagged in the corpus.

## Design takeaways

- **Fordyce skylight back and lighting:** use a teal, blue and green "swirl of water" field with leafy lead lines,
  a pale-yellow inner oval and a small **rose/lavender whirlpool** at the center. Frame it with **honey-amber
  opalescent rectangular panes** and **scallop-shell roundels** in dark rings. Add mermaid, swimmers, fish and
  frogs. The current amber ring should become the outer frame.
- **Tabletop as a bath-hall floor:** a white small-tile mosaic with a regular grid of dark dots (men's hall). As
  an alternative, the lobby's dark **crosslet motifs with a Greek-key border**. Tile tints come from the 1915
  wording: green, yellow, red and black on white.
- **Marble rails and frames:** gray-veined white marble ("the grays of the marble") for card-table rails or the
  foundation frame. Use **pink marble** for accents (the Fordyce stairs) and **verde antique** (deep green, as
  under the DeSoto fountain) for a rare premium trim.
- **Metals:** **oxidized silver** for light fixtures and hardware, **solid nickel** for fittings, and **brass** for
  Buckstaff lettering, rails and Superior hardware. In 1915 the Fordyce did not use gilt brass, so mix
  silver-nickel and brass instead of using all gold.
- **Lighting mood:** "alabaster bowls" with "old ivory tints" give a soft, diffused, warm-white overhead
  pool. The Maurice sun parlor's **"soft amber glow"** under amber glass panels suits an evening or "lamp" table
  theme.
- **Card-room (felt) table:** the only documented game-table color is the **green** billiard pockets of the
  Fordyce gentlemen's parlor (1916). The cloth color is not documented, though the restored parlor shows green
  cloth. Pair it with dark walnut or mahogany wood (Maurice Den walnut wainscot;
  Buckstaff mahogany with **dark green leather**) and green-and-gold drapery accents.
- **Quapaw back (re-keyed):** buff/tan and cream chevrons with tiny gold and blue diamonds, an **ochre/gold band
  of diamonds and rosettes**, a turquoise inset panel with a cream winged-beast silhouette, and a dark bronze
  lantern.
- **Water-lily motif:** both the Fordyce lobby transoms ("cool green pools ... water lillies") and the Maurice
  pack-room glass (white lily on green pads, amber border, opalescent lavender panes) use it. It suits pip
  ornaments, the ace of hearts or a card-back border.
- **Assembly-room glass vault:** pale amber diagonal-grid panes with jewel-colored oval medallions (red, gold and
  blue, "studded with jewels"). The five panels represent "Music, Art, Science, etc.", which suits court-card
  backgrounds or a four-suit emblem set (violin, French horn, harp).
- **Terra-cotta sea ornament:** shells, starfish, dolphins, mermaids, aquatic birds and a **Neptune head**
  (Fordyce window surrounds). Use these for corner flourishes or the joker and ace frames.
- **Card faces:** follow the period US convention of poker size (3½ × 2½ in, a 1.4:1 ratio) with **large or
  "jumbo" indices** (USPCC since 1895). Courts are double-headed and colored **red, blue, yellow and black** (the
  Goodall lineage). A **linen-texture** overlay is period-plausible for the mid-1920s on.
- **Paint ground for UI panels:** apple green (dressing rooms), desert sand (lobby c.1940) and Pompeian green
  (1950s iron). Use the 1910s–20s names for the main look and keep the later ones for a "late-era" theme.
